For the open hyphen question, I reproduced `a 5 g-sample` blocking on `g` and `2 h-long` blocking on `h`.
**Case for BLOCKER:** Treating the entire hyphenated continuation as one token gives a deterministic rule that cannot silently accept truncated compound units. It avoids making correctness depend on an inevitably incomplete dictionary of known unit fragments.
**Case for ADVISORY:** In ordinary technical prose, `g-sample` and `h-long` express grammatical attachment, so refusing the correct physical unit creates a non-overridable obstacle to valid writing. Advisory treatment would expose that lexical ambiguity to the independent auditor while acknowledging that an unknown continuation does not establish a wrong citation.
**Do not merge — the single most important reason is that signed exponent notation still lets an incorrect numeric prefix PASS the complete science profile.**
